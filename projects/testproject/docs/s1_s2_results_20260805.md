# S1 and S2 Results: The Reach IS Object-Directed, And A Third Camera Hurt

Last updated: 2026-08-05
Both experiments ran on a **validated** serving stack (the trust exam passed the
same day - `new_machine_local_serving_20260804.md` Section 5), so both count as
evidence rather than as suspicion.
Baseline: `sim_place_data_generation_20260805.md` (4 runs, stable hover 13-18 cm).

---

## 1. S1 - MOVE THE ORANGE. Result: IT FOLLOWS.

**Question.** Pi05 reaches to ~15 cm and stops. Is it looking at the orange, or
replaying a trajectory toward a remembered spot? Those are indistinguishable
from the outside and imply very different things.

**Method.** Shift ALL THREE oranges +0.15 m in y and re-run. All three, so
"nearest orange" stays meaningful. One variable; nothing else changed.

```text
Orange001  (2.126, -0.304, 0.924) -> (2.126, -0.154, 0.924)
Orange002  (2.249, -0.332, 0.924) -> (2.249, -0.182, 0.924)
Orange003  (2.154, -0.405, 0.923) -> (2.154, -0.255, 0.923)
```

**Result** (length-matched, 900 steps each):

```text
orange y shift               +0.1500 m
arm mean ee_y shift          +0.0953 m   =  64% of the object displacement
arm settled (last 300 steps) +0.1166 m   =  78%
a positional prior would have given ~0.
```

```text
=> THE REACH IS OBJECT-DIRECTED. It is no longer "it reaches" - IT REACHES FOR
   THE ORANGE.

WHAT THAT ESTABLISHES: 012000 retains VISUAL GROUNDING - it locates the target
from camera images - and that survived a room-scale domain shift: a rendered
kitchen it never trained in, different lighting, a synthetic orange, and the
`top` camera absent and masked.

=> THE FAILURE IS NOT PERCEPTION. IT IS FINAL POSITIONING.
   It knows where the orange is and stops 13-15 cm short.
```

Consistent detail: tracking is 64-78%, not 100%, and `d_min_mean` rose from
0.166 to 0.249 in the moved configuration. That is a **systematic offset that
worsens away from the trained position** - not confusion about location. The
settled figure exceeding the whole-run figure suggests it converges toward the
object over time rather than drifting off.

## 2. S2 - ADD A `top` CAMERA. Result: IT HURT.

**Question.** The 2-camera runs mask `top` (padded with -1). How much of the
positioning failure is the missing view versus the domain gap?

**Method.** Added a third `TiledCameraCfg` to the single-arm template - overhead,
looking straight down (180 deg about X in ROS convention), 1.0 m up - plus the
matching `ObsTerm`. Confirmed active: `cameras exposed by the sim:
['wrist', 'front', 'top']`.

**Result** (900 steps each):

```text
run              closest   d_min_mean   time within 0.20 m   gripper range
2-cam baseline    0.133      0.166             86%              1.395
3-cam (S2)        0.136      0.228             23%              1.170
```

```text
=> THE THIRD CAMERA MADE IT WORSE. Closest approach is unchanged (13.3 vs
   13.6 cm) but time spent near the orange collapsed from 86% to 23%.
```

### Reading it against the pre-registered prediction

The prediction was: *hover distance changes -> camera geometry implicated;
unchanged -> look to appearance/lighting/physics.* It changed - **but in the
wrong direction**, which the prediction did not anticipate. So the honest
conclusion is NOT "camera geometry is the culprit". It is:

```text
A THIRD VIEW ONLY HELPS IF IT RESEMBLES WHAT THE MODEL TRAINED ON.
The pose used here was INVENTED (overhead, straight down, 1.0 m). That is *a*
top camera, not *our* top camera. The model now has a `top` slot filled with
real pixels that match nothing in its training distribution.

A MASKED view it can ignore. A WRONG view it apparently cannot.
```

```text
CONSEQUENCE FOR THE SIM-FIRST PLAN: the top-camera pose is NOT a free parameter.
sim_first_strategy_20260805.md says to choose the sim pose deliberately and
build the real rig to match. That still holds for a RETRAINED model. But for
EVALUATING 012000 - which was trained with a specific real top camera - the sim
pose must approximate THAT camera, or the third input is worse than none.

The two cases pull in opposite directions and must be kept separate:
  evaluating 012000      -> match the pose it was TRAINED with
  training a new policy  -> choose a pose freely, then build the rig to match
```

## 3. What These Two Change

```text
BEFORE: "Pi05 reaches 5-9 cm in an unseen sim kitchen; unknown whether it is
         object-directed; unknown whether the missing camera matters."

AFTER:  the reach IS object-directed (S1, 64-78% tracking)
        perception survived the domain shift; FINAL POSITIONING is the failure
        adding an arbitrary third camera HURTS (S2, 86% -> 23% near-object time)
        => the fix is not "add a camera". A wrong view is worse than none.
```

Still true and unchanged: **sim2real transfer is unproven.** Everything here is
measured in simulation on a checkpoint trained on real data. None of it says
what the arm will do on the table.

## 5. Open Questions These Raise

```text
What top-camera pose approximates our REAL C270? Needed before a third view can
  help 012000. Measure the physical mount rather than guess again.
Is the 64-78% tracking a scale error, a depth error, or a learned offset?
  Moving the oranges in x, and in z, would separate those.
Does the systematic offset shrink if the sim scene is made to look more like our
  table (lighting, texture, orange appearance)?
Would a policy TRAINED on 3-camera sim data use the third view properly?
  That is the S5/GR00T path, and it is a different question from evaluating a
  2-camera-trained checkpoint with a third view bolted on.
```
